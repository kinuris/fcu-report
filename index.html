Abstract & Executive Summary

This institutional study quantifies customer and student satisfaction data across 38 operational workbooks at Filamer Christian University, Inc. (FCU), representing a verified census of N = 12,679 completed submissions. The university achieves an aggregate Mean Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT) of 4.37 out of 5.00 (87.4% favorable index; Top-2-Box satisfaction of 88.0%) and an overall Net Promoter Score (NPS) of +55.5. Across the eight standardized service dimensions grounded in Republic Act No. 11032, ISO 9001:2015 Clause 9.1.2, and ISO 21001:2025 Clause 9.1.2 (excluding 7 non-conforming departmental instruments with specialized rubrics, N = 5,757), Integrity (4.50), Assurance (4.50), and Outcome (4.50) emerged as core institutional strengths, with Communication (4.46) and operational Reliability (4.45) demonstrating strong baseline performance, while Costs & Transparency (4.38) and transaction Responsiveness (4.39) represent priority areas for workflow enhancement. Quality Management Audit: A formal audit of all 38 source datasets against ISO 9001:2015 Clause 9.1.2 (Customer Satisfaction), ISO 21001:2025 Clause 9.1.2 (Satisfaction of Learners, Other Beneficiaries, and Staff), and statutory Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) mandates revealed that 25 units (65.8%) conform to the institutional standard. Seven units exhibit critical construct or statutory non-conformances, while six units feature template defects, omitted dimensions, or quiz contamination requiring Quality Assurance (QA) remediation.

Institutional Corrective Action Plan

To ensure university-wide readiness for future institutional accreditation (PAASCU/PACUCOA) and third-party ISO 9001:2015 and ISO 21001:2025 surveillance audits and re-certifications, the MIS Office issues the following binding corrective directives:

  1. Mandatory Master Instrument Lock: Standardize all operational customer satisfaction forms onto the certified 18-column university master instrument. Units may not delete, truncate, or rewrite the core 8 ARTA/ISO dimensions.
  2. Scale Standardization: Ensure all recommendation items utilize the certified 0–10 Net Promoter scale rather than truncated 1–5 scales to enable valid institutional benchmarking.
  3. Statutory Data Privacy Gate: Mandate a non-bypassable Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) consent checkbox as Question 1 on all public Google Forms prior to collecting any transactional ratings.
  4. Separation of Curricular vs. Transactional Evaluations: Maintain separate Google Forms for academic course/curriculum evaluations versus operational front-line service delivery.

Section V: Methodological Framework & Psychometric Rationale

This research paper employs a multi-tiered quantitative framework synthesizing psychometric scaling, customer loyalty segmentation, and statutory quality management standards.

Net Promoter Segmentation (Promoters, Passives, and Detractors)

The Net Promoter Score (NPS) framework was formulated by Frederick F. Reichheld (Bain & Company) and published in the Harvard Business Review (2003, "The One Number You Need to Grow") and expanded in The Ultimate Question 2.0 (2011). In higher education (Browning, 2018), NPS serves as a leading indicator of institutional advocacy, student persistence, and alumni engagement.

Stakeholders respond to the standardized likelihood-to-recommend item on an 11-point continuum (0 to 10). Responses are categorized into three behavioral archetypes:

  • Promoters (Scores 9–10): Highly loyal brand champions who exhibit active positive word-of-mouth and retention.
  • Passives (Scores 7–8): Satisfied but unenthusiastic stakeholders who are vulnerable to competitive disengagement.
  • Detractors (Scores 0–6): Dissatisfied stakeholders who experienced procedural friction or poor service. Behavioral research indicates that scores ≤ 6 account for over 80% of institutional complaints.
NPS Mathematical Formulation
NPS = ( NPromoters NTotal × 100 ) − ( NDetractors NTotal × 100 ) = % Promoters − % Detractors
Where Promoters = scores 9–10, Passives = scores 7–8, and Detractors = scores 0–6 on a verified 0–10 continuum.

5-Point Likert Rating Scale Psychometrics

Developed by Rensis Likert (1932), the 5-point summated rating scale is the global standard for socio-psychological and customer satisfaction measurement. The scale balances psychological resolution while minimizing respondent cognitive fatigue:

5-Point Summated Likert Continuum
1 = Strongly Disagree • 2 = Disagree • 3 = Neutral • 4 = Agree • 5 = Strongly Agree

To facilitate accreditation self-surveys (e.g., PAASCU, PACUCOA) and executive oversight, evaluation scores are interpreted against formal institutional benchmark tiers:

  • Excellence Tier (4.50 – 5.00): Best-in-class operational performance.
  • Standard Quality Tier (4.00 – 4.49): Normal institutional compliance meeting stakeholder expectations.
  • Priority Focus Tier (< 4.00): Sub-par operational delivery requiring immediate workflow intervention.

Top-2-Box Satisfaction Metric

The Top-2-Box index reflects the combined percentage of respondents who selected the top two positive rating categories (Scores 4 and 5: Agree and Strongly Agree).

Top-2-Box Favorable Proportion Formulation
Top-2-Box % = ( f4 + f5 ∑i=15 fi ) × 100 = ( Frequency of Agree (4) + Strongly Agree (5) Total Valid Response Frequency (1 to 5) ) × 100

In consumer and service management literature (Morgan & Rego, 2006; Farris et al., 2010), Top-2-Box provides a robust safeguard against skewed arithmetic means caused by outlier distributions.

Regulatory & International Standards Governance

The survey instruments are aligned with:

  • Republic Act No. 11032 (Ease of Doing Business & Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018): Codifies the eight fundamental transactional service dimensions: Responsiveness, Reliability, Access & Facilities, Communication, Costs, Integrity, Assurance, and Outcome.
  • ISO 9001:2015 Clause 9.1.2 (Customer Satisfaction): Mandates systematic monitoring of customer perceptions of the degree to which requirements have been met.
  • ISO 21001:2025 Clause 9.1.2 (Satisfaction of Learners, Other Beneficiaries, and Staff): Prescribes requirements for educational institutions to continuously evaluate the satisfaction of learners, faculty, parents, and community beneficiaries through validated feedback instruments.
  • Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012): Mandates express, informed consent before collecting identifiable stakeholder information.
